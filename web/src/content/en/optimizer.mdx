# AdamW

The exact update, in PyTorch's order of operations:

$$
\theta \leftarrow \theta(1 - \gamma\lambda)
$$

$$
m \leftarrow \beta_1 m + (1{-}\beta_1)g \qquad v \leftarrow \beta_2 v + (1{-}\beta_2)g^2
$$

$$
\theta \leftarrow \theta - \gamma\,\frac{\hat m}{\sqrt{\hat v} + \varepsilon},
\qquad \hat m = \tfrac{m}{1-\beta_1^t},\; \hat v = \tfrac{v}{1-\beta_2^t}
$$

<Junior>
  Plain gradient descent takes steps proportional to the gradient — huge
  where the surface is steep, nothing where it's flat. Adam keeps a running
  average of each weight's gradient (momentum, $m$) and of its square
  (a per-weight volume knob, $v$), so every weight gets a step of roughly
  the same size in the right direction. Look at the Δθ heatmap: almost all
  cells are ±lr.
</Junior>

<Senior>
  At $t{=}1$ the bias corrections cancel exactly: $\hat m = g$,
  $\hat v = g^2$, so $\Delta\theta \approx -\gamma\,\mathrm{sign}(g)$ —
  Adam's first step is sign descent, which is why the Δ panel is nearly
  binary. The $\varepsilon$ outside the square root (AdamW/PyTorch
  convention) only matters where $|g| \lesssim \varepsilon$.

  The **W** is decoupled weight decay: $\theta$ shrinks multiplicatively
  *before* the update, instead of adding $\lambda\theta$ into $g$ where the
  $1/\sqrt{\hat v}$ normalization would distort it — the fix that made
  weight decay meaningful for adaptive optimizers (Loshchilov & Hutter).
  Optimizer state is 2× the model: $m$ and $v$ are each another full copy —
  at GPT-3 scale, that's the memory bill.
</Senior>
